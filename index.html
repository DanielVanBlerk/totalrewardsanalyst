A Worked Example For The Total Rewards Analyst Role · Daniel Van Blerk

Checking The Data, Comparing Pay & Planning The Budget

Organisations can change shape and structure, and pay transparency expectations are arriving at different speeds across Europe. In both cases the groundwork is quite similar with the groundwork being: reliable data, comparisons that are fair, and a budget that holds together. This worked example walks through each step, at a modest scale.

About The Data Used. The data used below is synthetic. The dataset consists of 480 employees, 6 grades, 3 job families, generated with a fixed seed. No real employee data is used. The next step would be to calibrate the spread to public figures (see section 5). The 5% review line is a screening level only, based on the EU Pay Transparency Directive, and would need checking against current national rules.

1. Check The Data, Especially After a Restructure

When teams and positions move, records tend to drift. A few simple rules catch most of it (see table below). Flagged records are left out of the figures below and passed back to whoever owns them.

2. Where pay sits within each grade

Compa-ratio is salary divided by the grade midpoint, so 1.00 means paid at the midpoint. Staying within 0.90–1.10 is a common rule of thumb. Comparing groups on compa-ratio keeps the comparison within the same level of work.

3. Comparing pay by job family, within grade

The Directive looks at groups doing work of equal value, so grade alone may not be justified. Here the comparison is made per job family. Groups with fewer than 10 people on either side are marked as too small to judge, since small numbers move a lot on their own.

5. What would make this more real

  • Public benchmarks. The UK Gender Pay Gap Service, Statistics Sweden and Eurostat earnings data could set realistic pay spreads and gap sizes.
  • Data owners. A short agreement on who fixes each flagged record, and by when.
  • Local specialists. Rules differ by country, so legal and HR colleagues would confirm thresholds and timing before anything is relied on.
  • Tooling. In practice this runs on HRIS exports, with Power Query for cleaning, Power Pivot for the model and Power BI for the view. That is the stack I use day to day, and SQL for checks such as duplicate IDs.
